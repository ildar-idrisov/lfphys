# S03's forces: sources, checks and measured effects — 2026-11-18 (EX2 step E03)

Purpose: the record behind the forces E03 added for S03's ball (EX2 design `explore_EX2_2026-11-18_design.md` §10,
§11 items 25, 39–42, Appendix A.8; the owner's direction of 2026-11-18, `../../AGENTS.md` §1): the body in a medium
(`core.mechanics.particle`: buoyancy and added mass), the Earth's frame (`core.mechanics.earth_frame`: the WGS 84
normal gravity and the Coriolis force), the history force's bound (`core.mechanics.drag.history_bound`), the spinning
sphere (`theories.models.magnus`: the Magnus force and the spin's drag) and the second method
(`theories.models.drag_quadratic.angle_flight`). For each source: the file it was read from (sha256, first 16 hex
digits), what was checked and how. Every file was downloaded into its own scratch folder with a generic user agent and
no e-mail or personal identifier in the request, converted with `pdftotext -layout`, and every quotation below was
found in that text (line breaks removed). Status per item: CHECKED (against the source's own text or table) /
NOT FOUND (no checkable source) / STATED (an estimate of this engine, with its basis).

## 1. The WGS 84 normal gravity — CHECKED

Source: «Department of Defense World Geodetic System 1984, Its Definition and Relationships with Local Geodetic
Systems», NIMA TR8350.2, Third Edition, 4 July 1997, with Amendment 1 of 3 January 2000. The official NGA download did
not answer; read in a third-party copy, `gis-lab.info/docs/nima-tr8350.2-wgs84fin.pdf` (sha256 831093a17398000d; its
first page is the Amendment 1 notice).

Verified (the engine's `earth_frame.WGS84`, `test_earth_frame.py`):
- Table 3.1: a = 6378137.0 m, 1/f = 298.257223563, ω = 7292115.0 x 10⁻¹¹ rad/s, GM = 3986004.418 x 10⁸ m³/s².
- Table 3.3: b = 6356752.3142 m, e² = 6.69437999014 x 10⁻³. Table 3.4: γe = 9.7803253359 m/s², γp = 9.8321849378
  m/s², k = 0.00193185265241, m = ω²a²b/GM = 0.00344978650684.
- §4.2, eq. (4-1), Somigliana's closed formula γ = γe (1 + k sin²φ) / √(1 − e² sin²φ), k = bγp/(aγe) − 1, φ the geodetic
  latitude: «Theoretical normal gravity (γ), the magnitude of the gradient of the normal potential function U, is given
  on (at) the surface of the ellipsoid by the closed formula of Somigliana». The radical is lost in the text output;
  the formula with it reproduces γp from γe, k, e² to 6·10⁻¹¹ m/s² (the printed digits), without it it would not.
- §4.3, eq. (4-3): γh = γ [1 − (2/a)(1 + f + m − 2f sin²φ) h + (3/a²) h²], «When the geodetic height (h) is small».
- §3.2.4: «This value represents a standard Earth rotating with a constant angular velocity. Note that the actual
  angular velocity of the Earth fluctuates with time.» (the Coriolis force uses this ω.)
- Consistency (tests): e² = f(2 − f), b = a(1 − f), m and k from the defining constants agree with the printed values
  to their last printed digit (k to 1.3·10⁻¹¹, inside the rounding of γp and b).

Results: γ(0°) = 9.7803253359, γ(90°) = 9.8321849379 m/s²; γ(45°) = 9.806198 m/s² (the design's 9.80620, «as
recalled»: confirmed); g_n = 9.80665 m/s² is the normal gravity on the ellipsoid at **45.4996°** (the design's 45.50°:
confirmed).

## 2. The Coriolis force and its first-order closed form — CHECKED

Source: K. Intriligator, UC San Diego, lecture outline of 1/17/20 («Reading: Taylor Chapter 9»),
`kintriligator.physics.ucsd.edu/w20/lec6.pdf` (sha256 aa7838c0063871b3). In local coordinates «ẑ points up …, ŷ points
North …, and x̂ points East», «Ω = (0, Ω sin θ, Ω cos θ)» (θ the colatitude), the equations of motion with the
Coriolis term and «x(1) = (1/3) Ωgt³ sin θ. So the object falls to the East.» The engine's Ω = ω (0, cos φ, sin φ) in
(east, north, up) is the same with φ = 90° − θ; its `drop_east_drift` is the first-order result, and the 3-D flight
with the whole force reproduces it to (ΩT)² (measured 5·10⁻⁹ relative for a 100 m drop).

Not used: Landau & Lifshitz §39 (not read here). The Coriolis force on a body in the air acts on the body's own mass;
the entrained air's and the rotating air's terms are of order ρ_air/ρ_body of the Coriolis effect (3·10⁻³ of it for
the light ball, 1.5·10⁻⁴ for steel) and are not included — STATED (their form was not checked against a source).

## 3. Buoyancy and the added mass — CHECKED (C_A to Re 300), STATED beyond

- Buoyancy: Archimedes' ρ_f V g of a fluid at rest in the same uniform field (exact; no source needed beyond the
  hydrostatic pressure).
- Added mass: J. Magnaudet, M. Rivero, J. Fabre, «Accelerated flows past a rigid sphere or a spherical bubble. Part 1.
  Steady straining flow», J. Fluid Mech. 284, 97–135 (1995), abstract (Cambridge Core page, saved 2026-11-18, sha256
  225352016c51fedd): «Computations are carried out over the range 0.1 ≤ Re ≤ 300 for flows around both a rigid sphere
  and an inviscid spherical bubble» and «the sphere undergoes an added mass force whose coefficient remains the same
  as in inviscid flow or in creeping flow, i.e. C M = ½, whatever the Reynolds number.» The creeping-flow form: the
  Maxey–Riley equation as written by Daitche (§4), term «m_f/2 (dv/dt − Du/Dt)».
- S03's Reynolds numbers (2·10³–1.1·10⁵) lie beyond 300; no checked source there. Sensitivity (engine): a change of
  0.5 in C_A moves the chute's range (1 m) by 0.026 mm, the launcher's (45°) by 0.195 mm and the light ball's (20 m/s,
  45°) by 46 mm of 28.4 m (0.16 %) — STATED, for the world's drawer.
- Test: without drag the flight is the vacuum flight with g' = g (m − m_f)/(m + C_A m_f), exact; the buoyancy's work
  m_f g Δy and the added mass's −½ C_A m_f (v² − v₀²) in their columns; measured 10⁻¹⁵–10⁻¹³.

## 4. The history force and its bound — CHECKED

- Basset's term: A. Daitche, «Advection of inertial particles in the presence of the history force: higher order
  numerical schemes», J. Comput. Phys. 254, 93 (2013), arXiv 1210.2576 (sha256 d6447656c8ecf4ef), eq. (1): the
  Maxey–Riley equation with the history force «− 6a² ̺f √(πν) ∫ (1/√(t − τ)) (dv/dτ − du/dτ) dτ» (the radical restored
  from the layout), «Here, v = dr/dt is the particle velocity, u(r, t) the fluid velocity, mf the mass of the fluid
  excluded by the particle, ν the kinematic viscosity». With ρ_f √ν = √(ρ_f μ) this is the engine's
  K = 6a²√(π μ ρ_f).
- Finite-Re kernels below Basset's: D. Jaganathan, S. Ganga Prasath, R. Govindarajan, V. Vasan, «The Basset–Boussinesq
  history force: its neglect, validity, and recent numerical developments», Front. Phys. 11, 1167338 (2023), CC BY
  (sha256 b3b3c7b17f14596c): «Mei and Adrian [37] proposed the following semi-empirical form for the history force that
  uniformly captures both early- and late-time behaviors», eq. (5), the kernel [(π(t − τ))^¼ + f(Re_p, t)(t − τ)]⁻²,
  and «The kernel in Eq. 5 reduces to the standard kernel at short times (t → 0).» The standard kernel there is
  K_B = 1/√(πt) («the standard form of history force with the Basset kernel»). For f ≥ 0 (Mei and Adrian's f_H; their
  paper, J. Fluid Mech. 237, 323 (1992), not read) the kernel lies below Basset's at every lag: so |F_i| ≤ K∫|a_i|/√(t−s)
  ds for every component — the engine's bound. No established law at S03's Reynolds numbers (design §11 item 41).
- The product integration: derived for the engine (weights exact for a piecewise linear acceleration, written without
  cancellation; `drag.py` docstring); tested against 2a√t, (4/3)t^{3/2}, 2·Dawson(√t) (second order: 1.4·10⁻⁶,
  3.6·10⁻⁷, 2.2·10⁻⁸ at 500, 1 000, 4 000 steps), the midpoint scheme of the V52 (u) test on the same history, a drop
  with linear drag and a vacuum throw in closed form.
- STATUS V52 (u) asked for the history force «as a declared force for creeping-flow worlds»: that remains open; the
  product integration it would use is now `drag.basset_integral`.

## 5. The spinning sphere: the Magnus force and the spin's drag — CHECKED at Re 600–3500 and 10⁴; no measurement between

The design (§11 item 39) required data covering Re 2·10³–7·10³ at S 0.3–1. Found and checked:

- **S. Zhou, G. Zhang, X. Xu, «Experiments on the Drag and Lift Coefficients of a Spinning Sphere», Water 14(17), 2593
  (2022)**, CC BY 4.0, `mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-14-02593/article_deploy/water-14-02593.pdf` (sha256
  d0ba4eb98acdba87). Measurements: glass spheres (2390 kg/m³; D 2.5, 4, 6, 8 mm) released on a track («orbit») 50 mm
  above a water tank; «the velocities and spin speeds of settled spheres were obtained by adjusting the release height
  of spheres on the orbit»; a camera at «400 frames per second»; C_L and C_D from the balance of the steady motion
  (eqs. (4)–(7): «when a particle moves at a constant velocity along a straight line, the acceleration of the particle
  is zero»). «Dimensionless angular speed is defined as Rr = DΩ/ur» (= 2S) and «Re = ur D/ν».
  - Table 2 («Experimental results», 22 runs, Re 610–3472) transcribed into `magnus.ZHOU_2022_TABLE2`; each run's Rr and
    Re recomputed from its D, u_s, Ω (ν = 1.0·10⁻⁶ m²/s) reproduce the printed values (test).
  - Eq. (8) (C_L, «0 < Re < 3500 and 0 < Rr < 12», R² = 0.873), eq. (16) (their non-spinning C_d = 24/Re + 2.5/√Re +
    0.4), eq. (17) (C_DΩ/C_d = 0.989 + 0.482Rr − 0.093Rr², «When 0 < Rr < 2.5 and 0 < Re < 3500»), eq. (18) (2.117
    Rr^−0.33, 2.5 < Rr < 25) — CHECKED, the code against them (test).
  - Caveat (stated in the module): their spheres' drag without spin is 5–20 % above the standard curve (run 3: 0.502
    against Morrison's 0.452 at Re 1496), so the force balance may carry a bias; the engine uses their spin factor on
    the standard curve, and the band includes their scatter.
- **M. Muto, M. Tsubokura, N. Oshima, «Negative Magnus lift on a rotating sphere at around the critical Reynolds
  number», Phys. Fluids 24, 014102 (2012)**, `eprints.lib.hokudai.ac.jp/dspace/bitstream/2115/49222/1/PoF24-1_014102.pdf`
  (sha256 c221ffbf81f4923d; AIP, «personal use only»: numbers cited, nothing copied). Large-eddy simulations; Table I
  at Re = 1.0·10⁴: Γ = 0.2: C_D 0.474, C_Lz 0.167 (s.d. 0.029); Γ = 1.0: C_D 0.661, C_Lz 0.301 (s.d. 0.096); without
  rotation C_D is printed as 0.046 (evidently a misprint for about 0.4). The definition is ambiguous: «the ratio of
  the circumferential velocity to the incoming flow velocity, Γ = D_p ω/U_0». Read as Γ = S, their drag over the
  standard curve (0.3926 at 10⁴) is 1.207 and 1.684 against Zhou's factor 1.167 and 1.581; read as Γ = 2S it would be
  1.093 and 1.378. So Γ = S here (a consistency check, not the paper's statement).
- Not obtained or not usable: Poon et al., J. Fluid Mech. 759, 751 (2014) (DNS at Re 500–1000; paywalled; values seen
  only in a figure reprinted by Sareen et al. 2024, not used); Tsuji, Morikawa & Mizuno, J. Fluids Eng. 107, 484 (1985)
  (Re 550–1600, paywalled); Sareen, Hourigan & Thompson, Int. J. Heat Fluid Flow 109, 109501 (2024) (forces only at
  Re 2.75·10⁴ and 4.13·10⁴, in figures; it reports «A relatively sudden drop was observed in both the lift and drag
  coefficients at a ‘critical’» rotation rate — not seen in the data used here at Re ≤ 3500); Poon et al., CFD 2009
  conference paper (no rotating C_L printed). Search log: `s03_forces_2026-11-18/magnus_search.md`.

**Coverage, as the design asked.** Measured C_L exists at Re 2070–3472 (Zhou's runs 7–22), i.e. the chute's first part
of the flight (Re 2.0·10³ at the launch); between Re 3472 and 10⁴ there is no measurement, only the simulation at 10⁴.
The chute's ball reaches Re 6.2·10³ at h = 1 m. So the design's fallback applies in part: the model (Zhou's eq. (8) and
(17)) is used throughout, and the **stated band** is the spread of the checked values around it in S03's domain (Re ≥
2·10³, 0.25 ≤ S ≤ 1.05): C_L × [0.825, 1.230] (Muto's 0.301 at Re 10⁴; Zhou's run 19, 0.449 at Re 2070) and the drag
factor × [0.917, 1.084] (Zhou's runs 9 and 19). The design's bracket C_L 0.1–0.5 «until E03 fixes it from data»
becomes 0.25–0.45 (the model 0.307–0.365 on the chute).

**Spin decay — NOT FOUND, STATED.** None of the sources gives the torque on a translating spinning sphere at these Re.
Estimate: a skin-friction coefficient of 0.1 (about three times a laminar boundary layer's 1.33/√Re at Re 2·10³) on the
whole surface at the largest relative speed (4.66 + 1.5 m/s) gives a torque below 2.9·10⁻⁵ N·m on the 20 mm steel ball
(I = 1.32·10⁻⁶ kg·m²): at most 7 % of ω = 150 rad/s over the 0.45 s fall from 1 m. A linear 7 % loss moves the range
by ≤ 0.010 mm (engine, `test_magnus.py`). The model takes the spin as given.

## 6. The second method — derived here

The flight's equations with the velocity's angle θ as the independent variable (`drag_quadratic.angle_flight`):
M dv/dt = −W′ sin θ − D, M v dθ/dt = N − W′ cos θ (M = m + C_A m_f, W′ = (m − m_f) g, N the normal forces: the Magnus
force −sign(ω_z)·½ρC_L A v², the Coriolis force's planar part 2mΩ_z v). Derived for this engine; with a constant C_d
it is the hodograph (agreement 4·10⁻¹⁵–3·10⁻¹³ against its closed first integral), with the real forces it agrees with
DOP853 and Radau IIA in time to ≤ 9·10⁻¹¹ (T, L) and 3.7·10⁻¹⁰ (an 8.5 cm apex).
